Notes Direction Lab

Four ways to fall for your notes

Same app, same notes, four very different directions, from familiar polish to something nobody has seen. The left phone in each one is live: tap, swipe, write, delete.

Direction A

Meadow

A calm, editorial notes app where every note gets a hand-drawn, layered cover that matches its tag. The patterns are familiar and every pixel is finished, so the art can carry the feeling.

Try this

  • Tap a card. Its cover grows out of the grid into the note's header.
  • Move the pointer over the big card: the hills, pagoda and blossom drift in depth.
  • Swipe a card left to delete it, then bring it back from the Undo pill.
  • Search or tap a tag. The grid reflows to fit.
  • Tap +, pick a cover, write and save. The new note lands on top.
  • Edit in the note view turns the title and body into fields.
  • Delete a note and its scene's light goes out before the card folds away.

Palette

Type

Young Serif for the greeting and titles: warm and bookish, like a magazine cover line. Figtree for everything else, 10.5 to 14.5px, with tracked small caps for metadata.

The moment

You tap the Kyoto card and it opens on a spring. Its corners flatten into a header that stays rounded only at the bottom, and the sky, hills and blossom travel at different speeds, so the scene opens in depth. Behind it, home sinks back and dims. The covers also live: petals fall, steam rises, the cat breathes, rain streaks.

Trade-off

The beauty depends on the art. Five hand-made covers look lovely, but every new tag needs an illustrator. The motion is deliberately standard, so this direction is the safest and the least surprising of the four.

In Flutter

Each cover is layered SVG: one flutter_svg widget per depth layer in a Stack, so parallax is a Transform per layer driven by pointer or gyroscope. Card to note is a Hero with a flightShuttleBuilder: a SpringSimulation drives the rect, the corner radii and a per-layer offset. The living covers are short looping AnimationControllers that pause off-screen. Swipe uses Dismissible, and the reflow uses an implicitly animated grid. Feasibility: high, about 2 to 3 weeks with the art.

Direction B · Our pick

Softbound

Every note is an illustrated clay hardcover standing on a warm plinth: linen cloth with a soft sheen, a tipped-in illustration, its title stamped in foil. Spin the shelf, pick one up, and it opens into a real page you can write in.

Try this

  • Watch first. On first view a ghost fingertip demos the spin and the opening once. Touch anywhere to take over.
  • Drag sideways to spin the shelf. Flick it and it coasts, then springs onto a book.
  • Tap the front book. It flies up, the cover swings open on its hinge, and the camera dollies onto the page. The illustration flashes past as the endpaper, and you type straight onto the page.
  • Drag the front book down. It squashes and sinks into the plinth like jelly. Undo pops it back up.
  • Tap +. A fresh book drops in and bounces. Type a title to see it stamped in foil, then pick a tag to see it re-bound.
  • Move your pointer over the phone: the scene tilts and the light follows.

Palette

Type

Bricolage Grotesque for titles and the foil stamping: chunky and optical-size aware, with the same friendly weight as the clay. Onest for body and UI: round, calm, and set on a 26px rule grid so the text sits on the page lines.

The moment

You tap a book and it lifts off the plinth and flies to you. The cover swings on a real hinge and shows the illustration as an endpaper. The camera dollies into the spread, and the right page becomes editable without a cut, because the text fields sit exactly on the rendered page. Back closes the cover and puts the book back.

Trade-off

This is the richest direction and the costliest. Each tag needs an illustration plus a cloth, label and foil material set, and the real-time shadows and physical materials cost GPU time and battery while things move. The scene sleeps when nothing moves. Screen readers get the plain list, not the art.

In Flutter

There are two honest routes. (1) flutter_scene on Impeller for the real meshes, hinge and shadows, with a FragmentShader for the contact AO. It is powerful but still preview-grade. (2) Pre-rendered sprite sequences of each book (spin, open, squash) scrubbed by a spring, with Rive for the jelly sink, and the spread as normal widgets over a pre-rendered spread image. It ships everywhere today. Feasibility: 7/10 via route 2, 5/10 via route 1.

Direction C · Already a working app

Atelier

Every note is a sheet of handmade paper on a wooden desk under a brass lamp. You don't use the app, you handle your notes: pick them up, write on them, crumple the ones you're done with.

Try this

  • Tap a sheet. It lifts, tilts toward you, the desk blurs and a lamp glint crosses the paper.
  • Write. Real fields on ruled paper; fresh ink lands wet, then dries.
  • Tap or drag the + New pad. A sheet tears off and opens.
  • Hold a sheet for 0.6s. It crumples; let go and it drops in the basket, or flick it there.
  • Tap the basket. Fish the last ball out; it unfolds and keeps its creases.
  • Tap or pull the lamp chain. Walnut evening to pale oak day.

Palette

Type

Caveat for handwritten titles, Fraunces for body text, Manrope for the small UI chrome.

The moment

Holding a sheet and feeling it crumple into a ball under your thumb, then watching it arc into the wicker basket.

Trade-off

Less instant Dribbble-style gloss, and it needs a client who is open to a metaphor. It asks people to learn a few gestures, so we keep a plain button for every one.

In Flutter

Already built and running on Android: CustomPainter, GLSL paper/wood shaders, drawVertices crumple mesh, haptics.

Direction D · Wild card

Inkwell

Your notes are ink suspended in deep, still water. Each one is a living bloom with its own colour and silhouette, curling slowly and captioned like a work on a gallery wall. Open one and its ink fills the screen, then parts to leave a pool of clear water to read in.

Try this

  • Tap a bloom to open it: the ink floods the screen, then parts around a clear pool where the words sit.
  • Type in the open note: each keystroke pushes the shore of the pool outward and sends a ripple through the ink.
  • Tap empty water to let a drop fall. It blooms in a new colour and opens straight into the editor.
  • Drag a bloom to the bottom edge or long-press it, and it dissolves. Undo condenses it again.
  • Move across the pool: the ink bends away from your finger like a current.

Palette

Type

Cormorant Garamond italic for titles, set like captions on a gallery wall. Hanken Grotesk for the body and the chrome, small and widely tracked, so the serif and the ink do the talking.

The moment

Opening a note is a reading ritual. The captions clear, the dye swells on a spring and fills the screen in about 600ms, then the ink parts like water around a dropped stone, leaving a pool of clear dark water for the words. It keeps curling at the pool's edge. Going back pulls it into a small cloud again.

Trade-off

Slower to scan than a list: blooms are art, not rows, and past about eight notes the pool needs search or a list view. It is dark-only by nature, the shader costs battery while the ink breathes, and text over moving colour needs a scrim to stay readable.

In Flutter

A FragmentProgram ink shader (the same simplex noise, domain warp and ridged folds, ported for Impeller) painted by a CustomPainter, with uniforms for blooms, ripples and the finger. The dissolve grains and drifting motes become a particle layer drawn with drawRawAtlas, and the ticker stops when the pool is off screen. Feasibility: medium-high. The shader ports almost line for line; the real work is holding 60fps on mid-range Android (render the ink at half resolution and upscale).
