# Offers lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Likelihood and time delay score well.** The product exists and works: one click gives every page on four devices, with honest notes when a page fails. The buyer sees the result in minutes, not weeks.
- **Effort is low for the core job.** Sitemap discovery, lazy-load handling, hidden cookie banners and settled animations remove the manual steps buyers hate. Capturing pages behind a login without sharing a password solves a real problem many tools leave alone.
- **The parts of a stack are already built.** Schedules, pixel-diff alerts, shareable timelines, ZIPs and a 14-day refund cover several problems a buyer hits after the first capture. They are not yet packaged as an outcome.
- **The ethical rules (no CAPTCHA bypass, robots.txt respected) suit agency buyers** who put their name on client work.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The pain is episodic, so the market is weak on recurring value.** Redesigns, handovers and audits happen per project. A freelancer can buy Studio for one month, capture, and cancel; the $99 Lifetime makes that even cheaper for them. Only the monitoring use is ongoing, and it is not the headline. If buyers do not keep paying, $9/month cannot carry an always-on Chrome server plus storage.
2. **It is priced and described as a commodity.** The offer lists features and page counts, and the open questions are all about going lower ($4–5 Starter, India pricing). This lens says that is backwards. Nothing in the offer makes a buyer feel stupid saying no, and "full-site screenshots" will be compared on price with whatever else the buyer finds. Competitor prices are unknown; competitor research answers that.
3. **The storage cost works against the price.** This uses only the founder's own figures: a full Studio capture is 150 pages x 4 devices = 600 PNGs at 1–5 MB, about 0.6–3 GB. Keeping 100 such captures could mean roughly 60–300 GB per Studio user, and Lifetime keeps that cost forever with no further revenue. Whether real users ever get near that is an open question for the CFO.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which one buyer has the biggest pain and the most money: agencies proving a redesign to a client, QA teams, or people doing compliance and archiving? Who do you reach first, and where? (Consumer panel.)
- What does the buyer gain in money or hours saved? Could you sell "client-ready before/after report in 10 minutes" rather than "screenshots"?
- Would an agency pay $29 or $79 without blinking if the output had its own branding, client share pages and change reports? (Consumer panel.)
- What do competitors charge, and what do they leave out? (Competitor research.)
- What does an average Studio user actually cost per month in storage and compute, and what does that do to Lifetime? (CFO.)
- Why is the guarantee a standard refund and not tied to an outcome?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Pick one buyer and one outcome** (for example, agencies handing a redesign over to a client) and rewrite the offer, its name and the pricing page around that outcome, not page counts.
2. **Do not cut price.** Drop the $4–5 Starter idea. Test Agency at a higher price with client-facing extras (branded share pages, change reports) before deciding $290/year is too much.
3. **Make Lifetime truly scarce and safe:** a capped, numbered batch with a fixed end, and storage limits the CFO has signed off on.
4. **Lead recurring plans with monitoring** ("your website's time machine") so the buyer has a reason to stay after the project ends; only build changed-only storage if the CFO numbers need it.
5. **Get proof before anything else:** about 10 paying users from the chosen buyer group, with their before/after results used as testimonials, and an outcome-based guarantee.
